ASTROVAEL 30.0 - BIG UPDATE: LANDINGS, STATION, RAIDERS, TRAFFIC

LANDING ON EACH PLANET (30.0)
- The landing is now a long sequence, about 24 seconds, instead of a quick lowering:
  - An approach from high above, the ship slowing from 16 units to 6.
  - The ship passes through two cloud layers (unshaded planes with the planet's cloud texture, turning slowly).
  - A hover, from 6 units down to 1.5, then the final approach over the pad, to -2.5.
  - The touchdown, settling onto the pad, with dust rising from the pad once the ship is low.
- The landing gear extends on the final approach: four struts with feet, folded under the hull at first.
- The take-off reverses this, about 26 seconds: the ship spools up on the pad, lifts off in dust, climbs through the
  cloud layers, and rises out of the air. The ship then descends back to flight height over the pass-by phase.
- The surface has scenery from its world: forests of trees on Verdantis, mesas on the desert worlds, ice pillars,
  crystals, lava seams and the ocean foam (the last four were already in earlier versions).

MISSION LOOP (28.9)
Every mission now follows this loop:
  1. BRIEF at the station: the signal card shows the planet, the universe, the distance, the travel time at your
     speed, the survivor, the threat, and the rescue window. A 3-2-1 countdown follows.
  2. DEPART: the ship leaves the station; the camera follows the station as it shrinks behind.
  3. FLIGHT: a long trip to the planet. The route is not straight: the autopilot swings the ship left or right
     of the line and brings it back to the lane at the planet. (Mission 1 swings right; mission 2 swings left, and so on.)
  4. LANDING: the camera moves to a side view as the ship lands. The planet hangs in the sky.
  5. RESCUE: hostile craft in that planet's colours attack the survivor. Fire with F.
  6. BOARDING and TAKE OFF: the survivor walks to the ship with a real walking cycle. On take-off the camera
     drops low, the nose lifts, and the flames grow.
  7. PASSED: $10,000 is added and saved.
  8. RETURN: a fast warp back to the station, with the stars streaming past.
  9. DOCK: the station ahead grows as the ship approaches its docking arm.
 10. ALARM, then the next signal. The next mission starts from the station.

PROBLEMS IN THE 26.0 / 27.0 CODE AND HOW 28.0 HANDLES THEM
(From reading the code. The game has not been run, so the effects below are what the code does
on paper; please report anything different.)
1. The flight stops on arrival at Mars (destination_reached holds the ship). The route now never stops.
2. The route was fixed to Earth-to-Mars and Mars was the only destination. Venus is now the first stop,
   and the route continues through the solar system and into new galaxies.
3. The ship moved through world coordinates for the whole run, so single-precision positions grow less
   accurate the further it flies. The ship and camera now stay near the origin, and objects are placed
   each frame from their route position stored in double precision.
4. Hazards, pickups and shots were detected with physics areas that were moved by hand each frame.
   Fast-moving objects can pass through an area between physics steps. Hits are now distance tests with a
   sweep allowance equal to the distance moved in one frame.
5. The track was recycled from a fixed set of five segments. Track tiles are now generated ahead of the
   ship and removed behind it, so the route has no fixed length.
6. Save data kept best distance in a unit that did not match the HUD. The save is now version 3 and
   stores best_miles. Older best values are not carried over; money and energy are kept.

EYE COMFORT (28.3)
- The game is dimmer and warmer. Cyan and bright blue glow were the main cause of eye strain, so:
  - the HUD uses soft amber and cream on dark brown glass, not bright cyan
  - the glow (bloom) and contrast are lower, and the overall brightness is a little lower
  - stars, nebula and the galactic band are fainter and warmer
  - rails, markers, gates, pickups, the ship's engines and the shots use warm or muted colours
  - the warp flash and the speed streaks are warm and softer
  - the black hole's outer ring is a muted violet instead of a bright one

CRASH MENU (28.3)
When the ship is destroyed, a menu appears with four choices:
- RESTART (from Earth): begins a new flight from the start, as before.
- REPAIR TO PLAY (from this place): a 3-2-1 countdown, then 5 seconds of repair (sparks and a
  rotating ring around the ship, and the ship flickers while it is being repaired). The flight then
  continues from the same place, with the same energy and diamonds. The obstacles right in front of the
  ship are cleared, so the ship does not crash into the same thing straight away.
- SAVE GAME: choose slot 1, 2 or 3 (each shows its saved distance and time, or EMPTY).
- MAIN MENU.

PAUSE MENU (28.3)
- ESC or the pause button opens a menu: RESUME, SAVE GAME, MAIN MENU.

SAVE SLOTS AND LOAD GAME (28.3)
- Save a game from the pause menu or the crash menu, into one of three slots.
- On the main menu, LOAD GAME lists the three slots. Choosing one continues the flight from the exact
  saved place: the same distance travelled, the same nearest planet, the same energy and speed setting.
- A save records where the ship is. Asteroids, hostile craft and pickups are not saved; they are
  placed again as the flight continues, and the space directly ahead of the ship is cleared on load.
- Saving is not available during a black hole jump or a repair (the game shows a short message).

UNITS (28.2)

BLACK HOLES (28.2)
- Every 50 million km of travel, a black hole appears ahead (a dark core with an orange and violet ring).
- The ship flies into it. The jump takes exactly 2 minutes of real time, at any speed setting.
- It carries the ship to the next destination that is at least 29 million km ahead. Planets in between
  are skipped inside the jump. (The first jump goes past Mars to Jupiter.)
- During the jump there are no stars, planets, asteroids or ships: only long streaks and a countdown.
- When the jump ends, the ship returns to the speed you chose (Slow, Medium or Fast).
- The next black hole appears 50 million km after the last jump.

SPEED OPTIONS (28.1)
- The right-side box has a speed line at the bottom: "SPEED · SLOW (click to change)".
  Click it to show SLOW / MEDIUM / FAST. Choosing one hides them again.
- Keys 1, 2 and 3 also select Slow, Medium and Fast.
- Speed scales the whole universe: travel, asteroids, hostile craft, pickups and the warp.
  Slow = 45% of the full speed, Medium = 70%, Fast = 100% (the speed of 28.0).
